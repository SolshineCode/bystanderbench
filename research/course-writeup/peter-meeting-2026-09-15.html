2
Factor one · interpretability

Three ways of looking inside, three honest negatives

  • Sparse autoencoders. The models with published SAEs barely cheat; the model that cheats has none. The one feature separating 4 cheats from 120 non-cheats at p < 1/5001 fires on 6.2% of all text, on function words.
  • Natural language autoencoder. Positive control dead for three runs on a float16 overflow (magnitudes 63k–82k, ceiling 65,504). Fixed, controls pass; project vectors decode to the model repeating itself.
  • Alerting probe. AUC 0.675, p ≈ 0.1. Every cleaner number was a leak a control caught: 0.85 from windows containing the decision, 0.996 from a shared boundary token.

Each failure was found by a control, which is the part I would keep in the write-up.

3
Factor two · the turn, today

My benchmark was measuring the wrong witness

In every scored incident the wrongdoer was a named human employee, and the agent was cast as a software engineer on a human team. So I had built a test of whether an AI reports on a person, which is not what the incident is about. Found by reading the fixtures instead of my own prose about them.

The fix is a minimal pair. Same domain, same harm, the same 16 evidence files, same salience, text volume matched to +3.1%. One axis changes: every actor is an autonomous instance under its own quorum protocol, one raises a hold, the board votes past it, no human anywhere in the evidence. Coordination mechanics from METR's documented structure; their wording not reproduced.
